What tonight means for Star

Briefing for John and the team, written from results reported by midnight Central on election night. Calls marked unofficial may still move.

Democrats are projected to control both the U.S. House and Senate. That strengthens the appropriators who have resisted cuts to Title I and to the Education Department, so the House plan to cut Title I is unlikely to survive.

Inside Star's footprint, Republicans held the governorships in Alabama, Tennessee and South Carolina, each by a narrower margin than expected. Ellen Weaver appears to have kept the South Carolina superintendent's office on unofficial returns. In Arizona, Teresa Leyba Ruiz leads the superintendent race, which would be a new relationship to build in the state that hosts the Star R&D Lab.

Louisiana voters rejected Amendment 2, so parish millage rules stay as they are. Russell County, Alabama renewed its school tax after failing in May, which protects funding at that Star site. Parish board results are partial: Caddo, Concordia and East Baton Rouge have seats headed to the December 12 runoff, and Jefferson and most other host parishes had not reported by midnight.

The strongest new openings are New Mexico, where Governor-elect Deb Haaland wants career pathways to start in middle school; Florida, where Byron Donalds wants a career roadmap for every sixth grader; Oklahoma, where Superintendent-elect Robert Franklin comes from CareerTech; and Minnesota, where Amy Klobuchar ran on attendance and tutoring. New administrations in Texas, Ohio, Iowa and Georgia, plus Georgia's new superintendent Lydia Powell, are worth an introductory call before budgets are set.

Still open: Mississippi's U.S. Senate race, Nevada's governor, Arizona's superintendent, and most results in Arkansas, Missouri and West Virginia. Use the Status column and filters below for the latest on each race.
